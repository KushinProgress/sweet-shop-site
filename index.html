Our Story & Heritage

Why Is Our Lassi Called "Badnaam"?

"स्वाद ऐसा जो बदनाम कर दे" — A taste so delightfully rich and addictive that once you drink it, you can't have lunch for hours!

In North India, when something is exceptionally good, people playfully call it 'Badnaam' (infamous). Decades ago, travelers crossing the Terai corridor through Pilibhit began stopping at our shop near Station Road. The thick clotted malai and slow-simmered rabdi piled so high on each kulhad of lassi that foodies jokingly warned each other: "Don't drink Badnaam Lassi before a feast, or your hunger for anything else will be ruined!"

Four decades later, that playful warning has become the undisputed hallmark of sweet indulgence in Uttar Pradesh. We still craft every batch with sacred dedication: whole buffalo milk boiled in massive brass kadhais, curd churned with wooden bilonas, and sweets made strictly with pure cow and buffalo desi ghee.

Wooden Bilona Churning

Traditional slow churning preserves the natural sweetness and velvety probiotics of fresh curd.

Pure Pilibhit Khoya

Simmered for 4+ hours to caramelized perfection for our signature Doodh Peda and Gulab Jamun.

100% Desi Ghee

No commercial shortening or hydrogenated oils. Every sweet and samosa is fried in pure clarified butter.

Terracotta Kulhad

Natural unglazed earthen cups absorb excess water, chilling the lassi and releasing earthy mineral notes.
